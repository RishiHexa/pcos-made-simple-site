In simple words

PCOS is a common hormone problem. Hormones are your body's tiny messengers. They tell different parts of your body what to do. In PCOS, some of these messages get a little mixed up. This can affect your periods, skin, hair, weight and mood.

Myth vs fact

Myth: If you have PCOS, you will never be able to have a baby.

Fact: PCOS can make getting pregnant take longer. But it is one of the most treatable causes of fertility problems, and many women with PCOS become mothers.

See a doctor if…
  • You think you might have PCOS but have never been checked
  • Your periods have stopped for 3 months or more and you are not pregnant
  • You feel very sad, worried or hopeless on most days

Getting checked early helps you get the right care sooner.

Questions, answered simply

Is this book medical advice?

No. This book is a helper, not a replacement for your doctor. Your doctor can examine you, do tests and give medicines if you need them. Never start, stop or change a medicine or supplement without talking to your doctor first.

Does the book promise a cure?

No. Right now there is no cure for PCOS, and the book says so clearly. It shows you how PCOS can be managed very well, with daily habits and good medical care.

What is the difference between PCOD, PCOS and PMOS?

People in some countries say PCOD and others say PCOS, and both are commonly used for the same problem. In 2026, PCOS was officially renamed PMOS. The book explains this simply, then uses "PCOS" because it is the name most readers know.

I do not know much English or science. Will I understand it?

Yes. It was written for women everywhere, including readers whose second or third language is English. The sentences are short, and every medical word is explained in one simple line, right where you see it.

Will the food ideas work in my kitchen?

They are built for many kitchens and budgets: rice, flatbread, bread, lentils, beans, eggs, yoghurt, vegetables and local fruit. No food is banned, and you do not need costly powders or special shops.

Can slim women have PCOS too?

Yes. The book explains that slim women can have PCOS too, and why your worth is never a number on the scale.

Can I print the worksheets?

Yes. Each of the 12 worksheets has its own page, so you can print only the one you need, copy it into a notebook, or keep a photo of it on your phone.

Is it useful if I am helping a daughter, sister or friend?

Yes. Chapter 19 covers explaining PCOS to family, talking to a partner, and helping a daughter or younger sister who shows signs.
